Turning a mission into a place people can find

Girl Power Foundation’s work with women and girls was mostly shared through informal channels. Donors, volunteers and partners needed somewhere reliable to learn about it and get involved. I led the project that gave the foundation its website.

Who the site had to serve

Three groups mattered most: donors deciding whether to give, volunteers looking for a way in, and partners checking that the foundation is real. Informal communication alone did not give any of them a reliable reference point.

From mission to pages

What the foundation needed to say

  • Why we exist
  • What we run
  • How to support us
  • How to join in
  • Who we are
  • What is coming up

Where the site says it

  • Mission presentation
  • Programme showcases
  • Donation path
  • Volunteer sign-up
  • Team profiles
  • Events section

  4. Launch

    QA, then go-live.

Beyond the website

As the foundation’s Project Manager since November 2024, I have led 10 projects on mentorship, education and careers for young girls. Participation grew by 50%, partnerships with NGOs and corporate brands added resources, and an advocacy event drew more than 150 attendees.

Streamlining how projects run also cut execution timelines by 40%.
