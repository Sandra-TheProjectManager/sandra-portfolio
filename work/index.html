    Head of Sales Operations / Project Manager

    Frutta SmartSeller — Implementation & Adoption

    Business requirements, commercial process alignment and vendor coordination for Frutta Foods’ SmartSeller implementation, followed by rollout, training and adoption support.

    Project details

    My role

    Business requirements, vendor coordination and adoption

    I owned
    • Business requirements and commercial process alignment
    • Rollout, onboarding and training
    • Adoption follow-up with sales leaders
    I coordinated
    • Implementation with the Autobridge team
    • Issue resolution during rollout
    Credit
    Software development: Autobridge
    Organisation
    Frutta Foods
    Focus
    CRM & SFA

    Frutta’s customer records, field activity and sales transactions lived in separate tools. SmartSeller was meant to bring them into one platform built around how Frutta actually sells. My part was making sure the requirements reflected the business, and that people could use what was delivered.

    Why separate tools were not enough

    When customer records, field visits and sales transactions sit in different places, nobody works from the same picture. Managers, area sales managers and reps each see part of the story.

    Frutta needed a shared platform that reflected its own route structure, pricing rules and reporting needs.

    Writing down how Frutta sells

    I owned the business requirements and the commercial process alignment behind them. That meant defining how sales, customer visits, outlet coverage and performance should be captured and reviewed.

    The point was simple: if the system recorded work the way the field actually does it, the reports built on top of it would mean something.

    Who built what

    Autobridge developed the software. I connected Frutta’s commercial processes and requirements with their implementation team, so that what was built matched how the sales operation works.

    From requirements to daily use

    1. Requirements

      Sales, visits, outlet coverage and performance defined with the business.

    2. Build

      Autobridge implements; I coordinate with their team and resolve issues as they come up.

    3. Rollout

      Users onboarded and trained on their own workflows.

    4. Adoption

      Follow-up with sales leaders on daily recording discipline.

    Making it stick

    A sales system only helps if it is updated every day. After rollout, I followed up with sales leaders on recording discipline and kept an eye on how the platform was being used.

    What was delivered

    A shared platform for distributor, rep and outlet operations, with requirements grounded in Frutta’s sales process and a rollout that included training and adoption follow-up. Application screens stay private, so this page shows the company logo only.

    Platform and working methods

    • SmartSeller CRM/SFA
    • Business requirements and commercial process alignment
    • Stakeholder coordination, training and adoption follow-up

    Project Manager / CRM–SFA Analyst

    Tolaram SFA

    Rolled out CRM/SFA across multiple sales territories, defining visit, route-compliance and outlet-coverage tracking, then training field teams until the reporting stuck.

    Project details

    My role

    CRM/SFA rollout, tracking structures and reporting

    I owned
    • Tracking structures for visits, routes, coverage and productivity
    • Training and change management for field teams
    • Operational and executive dashboards
    I coordinated
    • Deployment and adoption across sales territories
    • Projects with Sales, Distribution and Technical teams
    Organisation
    Multipro / Tolaram field operations
    Focus
    SFA · FMCG operations

    Across brands and regions, field reports were disconnected and summarised weekly. Route adherence, outlet visits and rep productivity needed a clearer, more current view. My work in the Multipro/Tolaram field operation focused on what to track, getting teams ready, and turning field data into review.

    Why the weekly view fell short

    Reports were split across brands and regions and arrived as end-of-week summaries. By the time a manager saw a missed route or an unvisited outlet, the week had moved on.

    Deciding what to track

    Customer visits
    Was the outlet actually visited?
    Route compliance
    Did the rep follow the planned route?
    Outlet coverage
    Are we reaching the outlets we planned to?
    Field productivity
    What did each day in the field produce?

    Bringing the field team with it

    A new system changes how reps spend their day, so the rollout was as much about people as software. I coordinated deployment across sales territories and ran onboarding, training, issue resolution and change management.

    The aim was steady CRM compliance and reporting discipline, not a one-off launch.

    From field data to review

    I built operational and executive dashboards from CRM, SFA, MFA and ERP data. They covered KPIs, territory performance, stock movement and sales team productivity, so field activity fed directly into operational review.

    Platform and working methods

    • Sales force automation
    • Route planning and outlet mapping
    • Field analytics and performance reporting

    Mplify SFA

    Field-sales automation for rep visits, activity and performance reporting.

    Project details

    My role

    SFA implementation, KPIs and dashboards

    I owned

    • KPIs for visit compliance, clock-ins and activity

    Focus
    SFA · Field operations

    Field work was recorded through manual check-ins and end-of-day reports that were often incomplete or late. That made it hard to verify visits, measure compliance or link activity to sales. I implemented and managed the Mplify SFA platform to change that.

    From reported to recorded

    Before

    Choosing what to measure

    Connecting activity to sales

    What it gave the team

    A structured, daily view of field activity, visit completion and sales performance for operational review.

    Sales operations & reporting

    Frutta Secondary Sales Dashboard

    The sales team operated without visibility into SKU-level performance. Decisions were made on gut feel, not data — leading to stockouts on high-demand products and overstocking on slow movers.

    Project details

    My role

    Data structure, KPI definitions and the dashboard

    I owned
    • Mapping the data from field reports to management
    • Data model and KPI definitions
    • The dashboard views and filters
    Organisation
    Frutta Foods
    Focus
    Reporting & analytics

    Field reports arrived in different formats, so simple questions took too long to answer. Which products are moving? Which reps are productive? Which regions are behind? I built a reporting view around those questions.

    The questions behind the dashboard

    1. Which products are selling, and where?

      SKU-level reporting

    2. How is each rep performing?

      Rep performance tracking, with top and bottom performers

    3. Which regions are on track?

      Regional achievement and territory comparisons

    4. What does each area sales manager need?

      ASM-level filtering

    The problem was structure, not collection

    I mapped how sales data travelled from field reports to management decisions. The data was being collected. What it lacked was a consistent structure and an easy way to reach it.

    So I designed a normalised data model and defined the KPIs the business cared about, before building anything visual on top.

    What the numbers mean

    MTD revenue
    Month-to-date sales, read against target
    Cases sold
    Volume, alongside revenue
    Outlet coverage
    Outlets visited, against the plan
    Rep compliance
    How consistently reps follow the expected routine

    The overview screen

    A note on the figures

    The numbers visible in this capture describe one reporting period. They show what the dashboard reports; they are not results I am claiming. Sections naming individual staff are left out of the public image.

    Where it helps

    Product, field and territory reporting now sit in one shared management view that each area sales manager can filter to their own patch.

    Tools and platforms

    • Advanced Excel
    • Power BI
    • Google Sheets
    • Python and HTML
    • Data modelling
    • GitHub Pages
